from fp8 to **bf16** (`VLLM_KV_CACHE_DTYPE=bfloat16`, `VLLM_GPU_MEM_UTIL=0.95`,

--depth D --runs 2`, `cache_prompt=false` (each run prefills the full

Notable: with bf16 KV, **prefill is slower at depth** (pp256K 953 vs 1563 t/s,
39% down) — bf16 doubles the K/V bytes read per attention step, so deep prefill
(attention-bound) pays a real cost. **Decode is faster at depth** (tg32 d256K
45 vs 34, d200K 50 vs 36) than the recorded fp8 figures — counterintuitive for a
double-width KV; likely reflects the fp8 run's smaller KV pool (0.85 util) and
scale-1.0 fp8 numerics rather than a bf16 decode advantage per se, so treat the
decode comparison as cross-build context, not a strict A/B. Coherence passed at
every depth (no NaN, no MTP token loops); MTP acceptance ~33% per-position
consistent with the fp8 era.

Bottom line: bf16 KV is the fidelity-safe default; the tradeoff vs fp8 is
**deep-context prefill/TTFT** (up to ~40% at 256K), which matters for long
single-shot prefills. If TTFT at the extreme deep end is the priority, fp8
(`VLLM_KV_CACHE_DTYPE=fp8`) halves that cost.
