# 2026-08-22 Qwen3.8-27B — depth A/B, bf16 KV vs calibrated fp8 KV

Complete 8-depth comparison of **bf16 KV** vs **calibrated fp8 KV** on the same
build (vLLM v0.28.0rc2, torch 2.13, AITER v0.1.20, `ROCM_AITER_UNIFIED_ATTN`,
MTP3, TP=2, 256K max-model-len, `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.95`,
`--max-num-seqs 2`). Both dtypes use the calibrated model copy
(`Qwen3.8-27B-FP8-kvscales`).

Method: llama-benchy 0.4.0, `--pp 2048 --tg 32 --depth D --runs 2 --no-cache`
(full prefill of `depth + 2048` per run, no prefix caching), thinking off,
single request. bf16 figures are the 2026-08-22 bf16 default sweep; fp8 figures
are the 2026-08-22 calibrated-fp8 MTP3 sweep (4K–128K) plus a fresh fill of the
missing 8K/200K/256K depths on the same calibrated build. Coherence **PASSED**
at every depth for both dtypes.

## Takeaways

- **Prefill/TTFT: fp8 wins decisively at depth.** fp8 pp is faster from ~16K
  onward and the gap grows with depth: pp256K 1547 vs 953 t/s (**+62%**), TTFT
  167 vs 271 s (**~1.6× faster** at 256K). fp8 halves K/V bytes read per
  attention step, so deep attention-bound prefill pays a real cost under bf16.
- **Decode: bf16 wins at depth.** tg32 is flat-to-slightly-higher for bf16 from
  ~64K onward (d128K 61 vs 37, d200K 50 vs 37, d256K 45 vs 33), whereas fp8
  decode decays harder past ~64K. This matches the earlier cross-build reading:
  the fp8 decode drop is tied to fp8's smaller effective pool / numerics at
  deep context, not a bf16 decode advantage per se.
- **Short/medium context (≤32K) is a wash.** pp and tg are within a few t/s
  either way; both dtypes are within noise.
- **Net:** bf16 remains the fidelity-safe default (indistinguishable quality,
  per the 2026-08-22 calibration A/B) with better deep-context decode. The cost
  is **deep single-shot prefill/TTFT** (up to ~62% slower at 256K). If TTFT at
  the extreme deep end is the priority, opt into fp8
  (`VLLM_KV_CACHE_DTYPE=fp8`) to roughly halve it.
