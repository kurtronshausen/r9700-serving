Observations vs the c1 depth sweep:
- **Full-context prefill scales well at c4**: `ctx_pp` aggregate is flat-to-higher
  than c1 across depths (11-12k t/s at d1024-d8192, ~6.7k at d64000), i.e. the
  server fills the batch with little throughput loss.
- **Decode aggregate is *lower* than c1** once contexts get long: `ctx_tg`
  aggregate collapses from 347 t/s @ d1024 to 3.6 t/s @ d64000. With
  `--max-num-batched-tokens 4096` and 64k-token contexts, the 4 requests'
  incremental tokens can no longer be batched together; each batch holds a
  single request's tokens and prefill/decode mix, so aggregate decode throughput
  tanks while per-request latency explodes (e2e TTFT 23.9 s @ d64000).
- `pp2048` incremental prefill aggregate follows the same depth curve as c1
  (~8k → ~2.2k t/s), confirming the attention-span-bound behavior.
- Short-context decode (d0/d1024) aggregates ~160-170 t/s ≈ c1's 188 t/s, i.e.
  near-zero batching loss at small depths.

## A/B: fp8 KV vs bf16 KV (c4, same stack)

Tested `VLLM_KV_CACHE_DTYPE=fp8` against the default `auto` (bf16) at the same
depths. Summary (aggregate decode `tg32 total`, and e2e TTFT):

| depth | bf16 tg32 | fp8 tg32 | delta | bf16 e2e TTFT | fp8 e2e TTFT | delta |
|:------|----------:|---------:|------:|--------------:|-------------:|------:|
| d1024 |     168.9 |    127.0 | -24.8% |        292.7  |       332.4  | +13.6% |
| d4096 |     103.1 |     69.4 | -32.6% |        973.0  |       895.0  |  -8.0% |
| d8192 |      93.2 |     68.2 | -26.8% |       1924.0  |      1750.6  |  -9.0% |
| d16384|      91.3 |     63.8 | -30.1% |       4048.4  |      3865.4  |  -4.5% |
| d32000|      62.6 |     44.8 | -28.5% |       9094.8  |      8549.0  |  -6.0% |
| d64000|      43.5 |     34.2 | -21.3% |      23488.4  |     21481.0  |  -8.5% |

**Verdict: keep bf16 KV (`auto`).** fp8 makes concurrent decode 21-33% slower at
every depth — the AITER bf16 `TILE_SIZE=32` cap (fewer/larger vs smaller tiles)
evidently serves decode better than fp8's TILE_SIZE=64, outweighing fp8's
halved KV bandwidth. fp8 only wins a small e2e-TTFT margin (prefill reads
fewer bytes). The deep-context decode collapse is **not** a KV-dtype bandwidth
problem.

## A/B matrix: deep-context concurrent decode (c4, aggregate tg32 total t/s)

All runs same stack (AITER unified attention, tuned MoE, froggeric template),

**None of the three help; keep the tuned baseline.**
- fp8 KV: -27% geomean (worst) — bf16's TILE_SIZE=32 cap beats fp8's 64 for decode.
- MTP2: -10% geomean — halving speculative depth doesn't relieve the collapse.
- 8192 batch: -5% geomean, but mildly better at d8192-d32000 (+1-10%); the
  `--max-num-batched-tokens 4096` requirement (gated-delta 2112-token attention
  block) keeps it from helping at the extremes.

**Conclusion:** the deep-context decode collapse at concurrency 4 is inherent to
attending over a very large cached KV per decode step on this stack — not fixable
by KV dtype, speculative depth, or batch budget. For >32k-token contexts at c4,
expect per-request decode ~40-85 t/s and e2e TTFT ~22-24 s. Serving deep-context
agents at c1-c2 (or shorter contexts) is the practical remedy.
