`patches/vllm-flashnext/43389-moe-int4-interleave-rocm.patch` already applied
(this is the baseline used below). Method: `llama-benchy 0.4.0`,
`--pp 2048 --tg 32 128 --runs 3`, thinking off, single request. Coherence
spot-check (single-shot + short multi-turn) **PASSED** with the patch applied.

Patch under test: `patches/vllm-flashnext/53899-short-conv-async-h2d-rocm.patch`,
sourced from a review comment on upstream vllm-project/vllm#53899 (that PR
itself is an unrelated CUDA-only PLE offload path; this fix is orthogonal
and applies regardless of PLE offload mode). Routes 5 CPU->GPU req-idx
tensor copies in `PleShortConvAttentionMetadataBuilder.build` through
`async_tensor_h2d` (pins the host tensor before the copy) instead of a
plain `.to(device)` off a fresh `.nonzero()` result (pageable host memory).
Reported upstream on 2x MI210 (gfx90a): took the profiled worker step from
33.2ms to 5.1ms.

## Conclusion

No measurable end-to-end tok/s change on gfx1201/RDNA4, unlike the MI210
(gfx90a/CDNA2) report. Both deltas are within run-to-run noise (same order
as the #43389 patch's own tg32/tg128 noise band). Consistent with the
earlier tg-slowness root-cause finding: on this stack, eager-mode per-token
compute (no CUDA graphs — blocked on the torch.compile
`ConstraintViolationError`) dominates decode latency, not this metadata
builder's host-copy overhead. The MI210 worker-step win (33.2ms -> 5.1ms)
evidently isn't the binding constraint here.

Kept the patch anyway: it's a strict correctness/perf improvement (removes
a synchronous pageable-memory copy for a documented ~290x-per-page-lock
cost on ROCm) with zero measured regression and no tradeoff. Worth
re-benchmarking if/when the torch.compile crash is fixed and CUDA graphs
become available — the metadata-builder overhead may become a larger
fraction of a much-shorter per-token step once the eager-mode floor is
removed.
